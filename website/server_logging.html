Introduction

There's always been this narrative that companies and websites are stealing our data. It's gotten so prevalent that we now have laws requiring us to accept or decline cookies, as well as trillion dollar companies having to testify to congress about what data they hold and what they're doing with it.
As I've gotten deeper into computer science, web development, and data engineering, I've come to understand what information you're actually giving away when you visit a website even when you reject all cookies!. The purpose of this page is to break down what data you're sharing regardless of which cookies you accept, why websites need this data, and what could go wrong if it falls into the wrong hands.

What Data Do Websites Need to Function?

Every single time you visit a website there is an exchange of information. The website needs to identify your computer, know what you want to do, and then send files back to your browser (usually HTML and CSS). The data your browser has to send to the website is called a request, and the server records these as request logs. An example of what one looks like is shown below: 
{
    "method": "GET",
    "path": "/differentiation.html",
    "ip": "203.0.113.42",
    "headers": {
        "host": "computermath.com",
        "user-agent": "Mozilla/5.0 (Windows NT 10.0; Win64; x64) AppleWebKit/537.36 (KHTML, like Gecko) Chrome/124.0.0.0 Safari/537.36",
        "accept": "text/html,application/xhtml+xml,application/xml;q=0.9,image/webp,*/*;q=0.8",
        "accept-language": "en-GB,en;q=0.9",
        "accept-encoding": "gzip, deflate, br",
        "connection": "keep-alive"
    },
    "query_params": {},
    "timestamp": "2026-06-03T10:23:41+00:00"
}
What we get here is a method (what the user wants from our webserver), where the user is trying to get the file /differentiation.html. We also have the IP of the computer making the request just below, which is used to identify who is trying to access your server. On top of that there are a few extra pieces of data, such as the website URL (host), the browser being used (user-agent), and the language the website is loaded in.
So far this is not that crazy. None of this is personal or identifiable information. I can't tell who this person is, where they live, or anything personal at all.

Why Do Websites Need This Data?

This data is essential to webservers as it can help ban malicious people from bringing harm to your website. Say a bad actor is accessing your webserver and leaving nasty comments like "you smell." You probably don't want this person getting access to your server files, or scaring other people away. So we want to block them while letting everyone else through. We can do this by IP banning them, where our webserver collects their request and then refuses to serve any of its files, preventing them from being mean to you or anyone else on your website.
